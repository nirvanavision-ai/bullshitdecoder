Exhibit I

The Heart

Human · whole, at intake
Donor unknown · condition — negotiable
Holding, or being held — undetermined

The first artifact is the one everyone arrives carrying. A heart, mid-gesture, caught between offering and withdrawing. We do not know whose it was. Every visitor who has stood before it has reported the same thing: that it looks exactly like their own.

It is assembled from dust because that is what a heart becomes when the sentences said to it go unexamined. The instruments in this museum exist to examine the sentences.

Exhibit I

The Quotation

Residue · of things said
Speaker unknown · date disputed
Meaning — undetermined

The most contested artifact in any relationship is the sentence itself: what was said, in which tone, and whether it happened at all. This mark is assembled from dust because that is how quotations survive — in particles, re-arranged by every retelling.

Hold it in focus and it sharpens. Let go, and it drifts — exactly like memory under pressure. The instruments in the next room exist so you don't have to hold it alone.

05

How to read anything here

It describes text, not people

No output concludes that anyone is an abuser. A flagged phrase is a pattern in words. Intent lives in a person; these instruments only ever see a sentence.

Mitigating evidence counts

Apologies, concessions, and hedging genuinely lower the reading. An instrument that can only escalate isn't an instrument — it's a mirror.

A clear result is not absolution

Harm travels in tone, history, and timing — places no text analyser reaches. If it felt wrong and nothing flags, your read still counts as data.

Every claim shows its source

Each pattern carries its lineage — Freyd, Stark, Sweet, Bancroft, Gottman — printed verbatim, so you can check the work.

An educational instrument. Not a diagnosis, not a psychological assessment, not evidence about any person's character or intent.
